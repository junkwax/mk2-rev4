# The MK2 input model

Generated by `tools/input_model.py`. Structure is read from the source
at generation time; the measured numbers name the rig that produced
them, because nothing in the source states them.

One tick is one display refresh at 54.7068 Hz — **18.28 ms**. Every
number below is in ticks.

## What it costs to press a button

Measured with `tools/sweep_addon_latency.lua`, eight trials a case,
zero variance on every row:

| | ticks | ms |
|---|---|---|
| button down to the move starting | **3** | 55 |
| button down to a hit landing | **9–12** | 165–219 |

The uppercut accepts at 5 rather than 3 because the duck has to
resolve first. Everything else tested accepts at 3.

## There is no input buffer

Measured with `tools/sweep_addon_buffer.lua`. A high punch, then a low
kick pressed K ticks later:

* **K ≤ 18 — the kick never comes out.** The input is discarded, not
  queued.
* **K ≥ 19 — the kick comes out at exactly K+3**, the same acceptance
  cost as from neutral.

So a punish has to be **timed, not held early**. Pressing during your
own recovery does nothing at all — there is no queue for the input to
survive in. Any advice built on buffering is wrong for this game, and
for netplay it means a dropped input is simply lost.

## The switch queue

Each player has a **20-entry** queue of `[switch, time]` pairs
(`sqs`, MAINEQU.ASM), written as inputs arrive and walked **backwards**
when a special is matched.

Three consequences, all of which look like the game ignoring you:

* **The port is read live, not latched.** `joystick_in_a0` (JOY.ASM)
  does `move *a0,a0,l` straight off `p_joyport` every time it is
  called, so two processes in the same frame can see different stick
  states.
* **Any extra button held kills the match.** `combo_look_lr` opens with
  `button_mask` and bails — *"other buttons pressed ---> NO !!!"*.
  Hold block, or ride a second button, and the motion silently does not
  register.
* **Matching is exact-state, entry by entry.** No partial credit, and a
  stray diagonal is never cleaned up — it sits in the queue as a
  mismatched entry.

### What each of those actually costs

`tools/sweep_addon_droprate.lua` introduces one hazard at a time
against a motion already measured as reliable (Reptile's spit, F,F+HP
at an 8-tick gap), four tries each. Attributing a failure in the wild
is impossible from outside — nothing reports which check rejected — so
each hazard is caused on purpose instead.

| what the player is doing | move comes out |
|---|---|
| nothing extra (control) | **4/4** |
| holding **Block** | **0/4** |
| riding a second attack button | **0/4** |
| **tapping** Down between the two forwards | **0/4** |
| **holding** Down across the whole motion | **4/4** |

Two things worth having in front of you:

* **You cannot hold Block and throw a special.** Not harder — it does
  not come out at all. Any second button does the same. This is the
  edge most likely to be blamed on the player.
* **Holding a direction is free; tapping one is fatal.** Riding Down
  through the whole motion costs nothing, because a held direction
  makes no new queue entry. A Down *tapped* mid-motion makes one, and
  a mismatched entry is never cleaned up. So a sloppy return through
  down-forward is harmless, but a deliberate extra input is not.

## Special-move windows

90 special-move records. The window is the record's first word —
the time the whole motion has to complete:

| window (ticks) | ms | moves |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | 292 | 7 |
| 24 | 439 | 10 |
| 32 | 585 | 7 |
| 34 | 621 | 1 |
| 36 | 658 | 10 |
| 40 | 731 | 2 |
| 48 | 877 | 8 |
| 52 | 951 | 1 |
| 53 | 969 | 1 |
| 54 | 987 | 1 |
| 56 | 1024 | 42 |

Tightest: `scom_decoy` (16), `scom_floor_ice` (16), `scom_lightning` (16), `scom_rd_melt` (16), `scom_hh_teleport` (16), `scom_noob_shadow` (16).

**That spread looks alarming and is not.** The window tracks how
hard the motion is, not how long:

* every 16-tick record is a **roll** - two different directions,
  which the hand does in one movement;
* a **double tap of one direction** (F,F or B,B) never gets less
  than 24, because the stick has to return to neutral in between.

Measured with `tools/sweep_addon_combogap.lua`, sweeping the gap
between the two inputs, three tries each:

| motion | window | gap that works |
|---|---|---|
| Raiden's lightning, D then F (a roll) | 16 | **1-10 ticks** |
| Reptile's spit, F then F (a tap) | 24 | **4-16 ticks** |

Neither is demanding: the roll accepts anything up to 183 ms
between inputs and the tap up to 293 ms. The spit fails at a gap of
**2** for the opposite reason - the stick has not been released yet,
so the second tap is not a second entry at all. **Release between
taps; speed is not the problem.**

So the only input in the game that is genuinely tight on timing is
the two-button one below.

**`combo_slack` adds 6 ticks (110 ms) to every one of those**
windows, online only (`combo_slack_online`).

## Two buttons at once

Kitana's fan throw and Reptile's forceball need both punch buttons.
`two_button_check` (MOVES.ASM) looks back through the queue for the
partner button and rejects it if too much time separates the two.

Retail wants the partner as the **immediately** previous entry and
within **2 ticks (37 ms)**. Measured with
`tools/sweep_addon_twobtn.lua`: the fan came out 3/3 at separations of
0–2 ticks and **0/3 at 3 or more**.

This build sets `twobtn_slack 2` and `twobtn_depth 3`:
the window is **4 ticks (73 ms)** and the partner may sit up to
3 entries back, so one stray entry between the presses no longer
kills it. Re-measured after the change: **3/3 out to a separation
of 4, 0/3 at 5**.

The trade: a wider window makes an LP→HP string thrown while
holding forward likelier to come out as a fan. `twobtn_slack 0`
restores retail exactly.

## Live switch values

| switch | value | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `combo_slack` | 6 | ticks added to every special-move window |
| `combo_slack_online` | 1 | 1 = that slack applies online only |
| `twobtn_slack` | 2 | ticks added to the two-button window |
| `twobtn_depth` | 3 | queue entries searched for the partner button |

All four are in `doc/balance.lock`, so changing one fails the build
until it is declared.
