# How this build differs from the arcade, and why

Generated by `tools/deviations.py`. **Switch values are read from
MAINEQU.ASM at generation time**, so this page cannot claim one thing
while the ROM does another.

This is a tournament build, so straying from the arcade is the point -
but every departure should be answerable. Each entry below says what
the arcade does, what this build does instead, what evidence says the
change is right, and what it costs. Anything marked **verified** was
checked by reading the retail source at
`Workplace/mk2-readonly/mk2-main`, not inferred from our own switches.

One tick is 18.28 ms.

## Active departures

### `dmgscale` = 1

**The arcade:** Every hit of a juggle does full damage. There is no scaling at all.

**This build:** The first hit of a string is full, then 3/4, then 1/2, then 1/4 for the fourth and beyond. A real hit never scales away to nothing.

**Why:** Rounds that end to one interaction are the complaint, and unscaled juggles are the mechanism. It resets on landing or blocking, so it limits juggles rather than taxing a whole round.

**Evidence:** Measured over 22 battles (`sweep_addon_phit.lua`): strings average 2.1-3.4 hits and reach 5 in twenty of them. At a median non-boss hit of 20 damage a five-hit string is 100 HP - 62% of the 161 bar - and 55, or 34%, with the curve.

**Cost / reverting:** `dmgscale 0` restores retail.

### `colwidth` = 1

**The arcade:** The hit column is the width `sq_punch`'s shifts leave: W - 2*((W>>3)+(W>>2)).

**This build:** The same width, measured out from the anipoint.

**Why:** Anchoring the column (see `anchorcol`) also changed its width, which was never the intent - `sq_punch`'s own comment claims the two branches keep the same quarter width, and truncation means they do not.

**Evidence:** Measured over every pose in the build: 757 of 869 (87%) got a NARROWER column than retail, mean 2.52px and up to 5, and not one got a wider one. So the anchoring fix also made the whole cast harder to hit - the opposite of its purpose. `tools/box_model_lab.py` puts the correction at 3.16% of all hit/miss verdicts.

**Cost / reverting:** `colwidth 0` restores the narrowed column.

### `backblock` = 1

**The arcade:** The block BUTTON is the only way to guard.

**This build:** Holding the stick away from the opponent guards as well, in addition to the button. Away+down guards low.

**Why:** It is PROXIMITY block, hooked into the question the strike path asks at the moment of contact and deliberately not into the stance loop - hooking the latter would make holding away enter the block pose instead of walking, which deletes backward walking from the game.

**Evidence:** Unblockables, airborne, low-block requirements and the CPU's own block path are all untouched, and when the button already says yes the new code does not run.

**Cost / reverting:** `backblock 0` restores retail. Note it interacts with `blockspecial` below: holding the BUTTON kills every special-move motion, holding AWAY does not, so back-blocking is currently the only guard that leaves your specials available.

### `joyskip` = 2

**The arcade:** Every step of a special-move motion must match the very next stick input, with nothing in between.

**This build:** A step may walk over up to 2 non-matching inputs before giving up.

**Why:** The switch queue is EVENT based - one entry per switch closure - so a diagonal does not make a diagonal, it makes TWO entries. The spare one lands between the pattern's steps and kills the motion, which is why a sloppy roll drops a move that a clean one lands.

**Evidence:** Measured (`sweep_addon_droprate.lua`, A/B in one session): tapping Down between the two forwards of a Forward,Forward came out 0/4 before and 4/4 after, with the control unchanged at 3/4 on both sides. Holding a direction was always free - a held direction makes no second entry.

**Cost / reverting:** `joyskip 0` restores retail. The first step is anchored - it may not skip - and Shang's skulls are ordered longest-first, both because without them a short pattern reaches past the head of a longer one and wins; `tools/combo_collision_audit.py` is the gate and Phase D fails the build on any new collision.

### `socdfix` = 1

**The arcade:** Whatever the encoder sends reaches the game. Left+Right and Up+Down are undefined, because a lever cannot produce them.

**This build:** Latched once a tick and resolved: Back+Forward to neutral, Up+Down to Up.

**Why:** Leverless controllers can produce both, and what happened then was whatever the hardware did. In the ROM rather than the driver because the netplay layer transmits the port - a driver fix protects one cabinet, this protects the match.

**Evidence:** It also closes a real hazard: the port is hardware and was re-read on every access, so two processes in the same tick could see different stick states. Covers BOTH halves: the held stick through `p_joyport`, and the motions, because MKINIT's switch scan resolves the port before it diffs against last tick - so the closures that build the special-move queue are resolved too. The scan resolves for itself rather than reading the latch, because the latch is refreshed by the dispatcher and could be a tick behind it, which would put a frame of latency on every closure.

**Cost / reverting:** `socdfix 0` restores the raw port.

### `blockspecial` = 1

**The arcade:** Holding BLOCK stops every special coming out - measured 0 of 4 against 4 of 4 clean.

**This build:** Block is dropped from the button mask, so holding it no longer rejects a special-move motion. Measured after: 3 of 4, matching the control exactly.

**Why:** It was off because a block that cancels into a special is stronger than what the game ships with - true when written, and untrue the moment `backblock` shipped. Holding AWAY guards and never reaches button_mask, so that capability already exists. All this does is stop the block BUTTON being strictly worse than the stick, which is a trap rather than a design: players would simply stop pressing the one input that is unambiguous about wanting to block.

**Evidence:** The restriction is an artifact, not a rule. button_mask exists because combo_look_lr matches queue entries by exact state and a held button disturbs that - the same class of artifact `joyskip` fixes on the direction side. Nothing ever chose 'you may not special while blocking'. `hk_held` stays 0 of 4, so only block came out of the mask.

**Cost / reverting:** `blockspecial 0` restores retail, and pairs with `backblock 0`.

### `saicool` = 040h*2

**The arcade:** Mileena's sai has NOTHING between throws. fn2_hp_open checks that HP was HELD for 022h ticks and nothing else, and a hold requirement is not a rate limit - hold six tenths of a second, release, repeat.

**This build:** A cooldown of 040h*2 ticks, the same one Kitana's fan has carried all along twenty lines below it in the same file.

**Why:** It was the only projectile in the game with no limiter of any kind, and it was reported from play as easy to spam.

**Evidence:** UMK3 is the obvious place to look and its answer was different: recovery there is 37 ticks against our 32 - five ticks, not the nerf - while the INPUT goes through secret_move_search(sm_mil_hpo), a real motion, instead of a hold-and-release. That is the better fix and was not taken: changing the input is a control change, and held-HP is also how Kitana's and Jade's boomerangs are thrown here. A cooldown reaches the same rate limit while touching no animation, speed or recovery.

**Cost / reverting:** `saicool 0` restores retail.

### `anchorcol` = 1

**The arcade:** The quarter-width hit column is centred on the victim's multipart BOUNDING BOX, which travels with his own limbs.

**This build:** Centred on his ANIPOINT (`oxpos`), which is stable under animation.

**Why:** The retail centre moves when the victim's arms do, so whether a move connects depends on the frame the *victim* happens to be showing. Retail already patches around this with a special case for `jade_nitro`; anchoring on the anipoint removes the cause instead of the symptom.

**Evidence:** Kept switchable so "did this stop a move connecting?" is a diff of two hit-ledger runs rather than an argument.

**Cost / reverting:** `anchorcol 0` restores retail.

### `combo_slack` = 6

**The arcade:** No slack. A special-move motion must finish inside the window its combo record names.

**This build:** **Online only** (`combo_slack_online 1`): +6 ticks on every window while `f_netplay` is set. Offline and on a cabinet the build is retail-exact.

**Why:** Netplay adds input delay that the arcade never had. The compensation is for the wire, not for the player.

**Evidence:** This was previously a bare `addi 6,a9` with a comment saying "netplay" and **no test of f_netplay at all** - so cabinets and local matches got it too. Measured across the 90 combo records it is not uniform: +11% on the 56-tick windows most moves use, but **+38%** on the seven that use 16 ticks, i.e. it helped most exactly where execution was meant to be hard. Gating it made the code do what its comment always claimed.

**Cost / reverting:** `combo_slack_online 0` applies it everywhere - a deliberately more lenient build, which should then be stated in the move list.

### `twobtn_slack` = 2

**The arcade:** Two buttons "at once" must land within **2 ticks (37 ms)** and the partner must be the *immediately* previous entry in the switch queue. (verified)

**This build:** A 4-tick (73 ms) window, and the partner may sit up to `twobtn_depth` entries back.

**Why:** This is the only input in the game that is genuinely tight. Everything else measured is generous: a roll accepts up to 183 ms between inputs, a double-tap up to 293 ms. Two-button moves - Kitana's fan throw, Reptile's forceball - were the one place a normal hand fails.

**Evidence:** `tools/sweep_addon_twobtn.lua`, measured before and after: retail comes out 3/3 at a separation of 0-2 ticks and **0/3 at 3 or more**; with the change, 3/3 out to 4 and 0/3 at 5. The depth part matters as much as the window - one stray queue entry between the presses used to kill the input even inside 2 ticks.

**Cost / reverting:** A wider window makes an LP->HP string thrown while holding forward likelier to come out as a fan. 2 ticks was chosen as roughly double retail rather than the largest number that felt good. `twobtn_slack 0` restores retail exactly.

### `secretwalk` = 1

**The arcade:** Smoke, Noob and Jade keep boss walk speed whoever is playing them - 4.5 / 4.0 / 4.5 px per tick against 2.5 for most of the cast.

**This build:** A HUMAN playing them walks at the speed of the character they share a strike table with; the CPU keeps boss speed.

**Why:** Smoke and Noob share `nj_strikes` **byte for byte** with the ninjas (all 32 records) and Jade shares `fn_strikes` with the female ninjas (all 26). Identical moves plus strictly better movement, with nothing in the data paying for it. Boss scaling exists to make a secret OPPONENT hard, not to hand one player an edge in a two-player match.

**Evidence:** The damage half of exactly this was already gated by retail's own `xlate_secret`, which applies the 3/4 scaling only when the CPU is playing them. `get_walk_info_f`/`_b` had no such test at all. This mirrors a fix Boon had already made on the other axis.

**Cost / reverting:** `secretwalk 0` restores retail.

### `rdshockai` = 1

**The arcade:** Raiden's electrocute grab (hold HP ~1.25s, release within 060h of a standing, non-blocking opponent) is refused outright unless the other player is a joystick. `rd_hp_open` calls `is_he_joy` and branches to `death` on a drone, so `do_raiden_shocker` is never reached in single player. **verified** - the two lines are in the leak, unchanged since the initial commit, comment and all: `; no zap on drones`.

**This build:** The joy test is compiled out. The move resolves against a CPU opponent on exactly the terms it resolves against a human.

**Why:** It reads as a bug to anyone without the source open: one of Raiden's specials silently does not exist in single player, with no whiff, no animation and no feedback saying why. Whatever the 1993 reason was - the `; sorry jeff !!` gate on the next line suggests a loop being papered over - that loop is closed by its own test, which stays.

**Evidence:** The move is self-limiting without the exemption. `do_raiden_shocker` checks blocking, airborne and x-distance (060h) and drops to `useless_shock` on any of them, which parks Raiden in `act_shocker_sd` - sd_punish_tier 3 in MKDRONE, so the drone punishes a whiff. The victim half was already exercised: `act_shock_sd` is reaction 613 in the drone table because the electrocute FATALITY reaches it (MKDEATH `raiden_post_shock`), and fatalities were never joy-gated.

**Cost / reverting:** `rdshockai 0` restores retail. The re-shock guard below it (`act_shock_sd` -> `death`) is outside the switch and stays in both states.

### `hitbox` = 1

**The arcade:** No collision overlay exists.

**This build:** `f_colbox` compiles in a CPU-drawn hit/hurt box overlay.

**Why:** A development and dispute-resolution tool. It draws nothing unless `f_colbox` is written at runtime, so it does not affect play.

**Evidence:** Documented in doc/HITBOX_MODEL.md and doc/UNUSED_GRAPHICS_PRIMITIVES.md.

**Cost / reverting:** Not a gameplay deviation. `hitbox 0` compiles it out entirely.

### `colnarrow` = 2

**The arcade:** A quarter of the victim's width is hittable. `sq_punch` squeezes the column on 205 of 223 strike records, so this is not an edge case - it is how the game normally decides a hit, and the rest of the fighter is scenery.

**This build:** A half. Same anchor, same everything else; the divisor moves 4 -> 2.

**Why:** The point-blank dead zone. Retail's uppercut starts 41px away for Jax and Cage, so standing ON someone and pressing down+HP whiffs - the column is behind the strike box's front edge. Losing a reversal because you were too CLOSE reads as the game breaking, not as spacing.

**Evidence:** Minimum connecting distance for the uppercut, retail -> now: Jax and Cage 41->28, Kung Lao 32->19, Baraka 23->19, Liu Kang 22->9, Raiden and the male ninjas 19->6, Shang 15->2, the female ninjas 9->0. Only the female ninjas lose the dead zone entirely; everyone else keeps a smaller one, which is why this is a dial and not `perpiece`. Far reach moves at most 1px. Over every move against every defender's stance and duck lanes at every distance - 10,968,880 comparisons - the quarter to a half moves 4.32% of verdicts, and it is PURELY ADDITIVE: 473,711 hits gained, 0 lost. Nothing that connected stops connecting. The high/low game is untouched, which was the risk worth checking: the same 119 of 199 moves whiff over a crouch under both, with the same median window. One behaviour-lock case flipped, and it is the one this is for: `uppercut_point` at dist=12 went dmg=0 (no reaction at all) to dmg=40 with the same 0503h launch `uppercut_mid` produces. Reproduce with: box_model_lab.py ranges --models retail build --move duckpunch.

**Cost / reverting:** `colnarrow 4` restores retail's quarter.

### `chipfloor` = 1

**The arcade:** Chip damage can kill - and it is worse than that. When chip WOULD be lethal, `strike_blocked` jumps to `block_is_useless` and throws the block away, so the victim takes the CLEAN hit instead. Retail's own comment on the branch is "block or no ---> he's screwed!".

**This build:** Chip cannot take the last point of health. The block holds, and the victim lives at 1.

**Why:** Not the usual chip-death argument. Retail does not just allow a chip kill, it makes blocking actively worse than standing there at exactly the moment blocking matters most - the same input that would have saved you at 20 HP kills you faster at 2. A tournament ruleset can argue either way about chip deaths; it cannot want the defensive option to be a trap.

**Evidence:** No behaviour-lock case blocks, so all 10 are unchanged, and the 25 battle stability sweep is clean. The change is one comparison on the lethal path - it cannot reach an unblocked hit.

**Cost / reverting:** `chipfloor 0` restores retail, `block_is_useless` included.

### `crossfix` = 1

**The arcade:** A motion is judged in whatever facing is true when the MATCH runs. The queue stores raw physical directions, each combo carries two patterns (a face-right copy and a face-left copy 16*6 apart), and `combo_look_lr` picks one from a single live `is_he_right` at match time - so every entry is re-read in the current facing, including entries made before a crossup.

**This build:** Each entry is judged in the facing it was MADE in. The physical verdict is stamped into the entry at write time and each entry is canonicalised to the face-right pattern using its own stamp, so the match-time `is_he_right` disappears along with the face-left pick.

**Why:** This is the crossup drop, and it is worse than it sounds because the fighter has not turned round when it happens and will not for a while: `turn_around` is an ~8 tick animation that disables buttons, and it is only ever attempted from the stance loop, walking or blocking - nothing even tries while airborne or mid-move. So the two facings disagree for the whole arc of a crossup, and a motion entered before the cross is judged after it. Jumping over someone and throwing a special is core fighting-game vocabulary; MK2 drops it.

**Evidence:** It is a NO-OP unless facing changes mid-motion: if every entry carries the same stamp, canonicalising them all is identical to picking that pattern once, which is what retail does. Only a motion SPANNING a cross behaves differently - that is both the point and the whole risk surface. Only the joystick CLOSE queues are stamped: `switch_queue_a0` serves the button queues too and `two_button_check` recovers a button with a bare `srl 16`, so a stamp there would break Kitana's fan and Reptile's forceball. `previous_q_entry` is left exactly as documented - two_button_check and get_last_button rely on the raw value and on it touching only a0/a6/a7 - and the transform lives in a wrapper only `combo_look_lr` calls. Stability sweep 25/25 and behaviour.lock unchanged at 10 cases. The crossup behaviour itself is NOT yet verified: no rig exercises a crossup.

**Cost / reverting:** `crossfix 0` restores retail, face-left pattern and all.

### `deferhit` = 1

**The arcade:** The first fighter whose strike_check runs in a tick wins any simultaneous exchange outright, and nothing ever trades. A connect calls `react_xfer_him`, which moves the victim into a reaction BEFORE that fighter's own process has run - so the loser's strike_check never executes at all.

**This build:** `react_xfer_him` records the reaction instead of applying it, and `apply_pending_hits` (JOY.ASM) runs them once the dispatcher has walked every process. Both fighters' strike_check therefore runs before either is moved, and a genuine simultaneous exchange becomes a TRADE - which MK2 has never had. Damage is NOT deferred, only the transfer: `bar_reducer` still runs inline, so hit counts, chip and the round-end tests are untouched.

**Why:** It is player TWO who wins, always, and it is structural rather than incidental. Proved from source: `getprc` links a new process after its creator (a13 is only dereferenced there, never written, and `xferprc0` touches a2/a12 but not a13); `setup_players` creates P1 then P2 from the same running process, so the list becomes [C, P2, P1] - the SECOND player created ends up FIRST - and `prcdsp` walks forward from `active`. Side assignment therefore decides close exchanges, which is disqualifying for tournament use. Swapping the creation order would not fix it, it would hand the same advantage to P1; deferring does not pick a winner, it stops the winner being picked by list order.

**Evidence:** Measured as a controlled A/B on one build pair, same rig, same mirror match (Baraka vs Baraka, both human, pinned at symmetric distances, both pressing High Kick on the same frame): with it OFF, P2 won 4 of 4 scored exchanges and nothing traded; with it ON, all 4 traded and neither side won any. Complete separation. The sample is narrow - four distances, one move, one matchup - so treat it as proof that the mechanism works, not as a survey. Earlier runs that reported 'trades favour nobody' were on a rig that drove P1's stick during fights; that fault is fixed (ADDON_OWNS). NOTE it cannot fix THROWS: a throw takes the victim through `takeover_him`, never through `react_xfer_him`, so nothing in the throw path is deferred. IT DOES NOT FIX THE KILLING BLOW, and that limit was found by testing for double KOs. Traced frame by frame in a mirror match at 82px: at 100 HP both bars drop 32 on the SAME tick (100/100 -> 68/68) - a true simultaneous trade. At 8 HP the same exchange gives 8/8 -> 0/8: P1 dies, P2 is untouched. The cause is `bar_reducer` (JOY.ASM), which opens by refusing to apply ANY damage once either bar is already zero (`jreq bred5`, 'zero already ---> mute point'), and it runs INLINE - deferhit defers the transfer, not the damage. So P2's check reduces P1 to zero first and P1's check is then short-circuited. The result is that ordinary exchanges are now even and the DECIDING one is not: at match point a simultaneous exchange still goes to P2, every time. Fixing that means letting both reductions land before testing for zero, which creates real double KOs - and `its_a_tie` (MAIN.ASM) is a bare `retp` that sets no winner_status and awards no round. So it is a chain of work, not a switch. Rig: tools/sweep_addon_dko.lua.

**Cost / reverting:** `deferhit 0` restores retail, P2 advantage and all.

### `dko` = 1

**The arcade:** A lethal simultaneous exchange is not simultaneous: `bar_reducer` refuses to apply ANY damage once either bar is zero (`jreq bred5`, 'zero already ---> mute point'), and it runs inline. So whichever fighter's strike_check runs first takes the other to zero and the second is short-circuited - and P2 runs first, by construction. The killing blow always goes to P2.

**This build:** The guard is narrowed to what it is for. `ko_tick` stamps the tick a bar reached zero; a reduction carrying that same tick is the other half of one exchange and is let through, while anything from a later tick is refused exactly as retail refuses it. A lethal simultaneous exchange therefore kills BOTH - a real double KO.

**Why:** `deferhit` evened ordinary exchanges and left the deciding one untouched, which is the worst place for it to survive: at match point, side assignment still decided the round. Refusing damage after the round is decided is correct; refusing the other half of the SAME TICK is not, and the two were the same test.

**Evidence:** Traced frame by frame, mirror match at 82px (tools/sweep_addon_dko.lua). Before: 8/8 -> 0/8, P1 dead and P2 untouched. After: 8/8 -> 0/0, both dead on the same tick. The outcome lands on `round_is_over`'s existing tie path - equal bars means `round_tied` - so it makes an EXISTING state reachable rather than inventing one (a timeout at equal health gets there today). Watched 600 frames hands-off afterwards: both bars refill, neither player is awarded the round, `winner_status` is untouched, and the round replays. No hang. Note `gstate` stays 2 throughout and no round is awarded - a draw produces neither signal, so do not read those as a stall.

**Cost / reverting:** `dko 0` restores retail: the first fighter resolved wins outright.

### `rollpunish` = 1

**The arcade:** Mileena's ball roll (`do_fn2_kano`, MKPROP.ASM - Khameleon borrows it) is safe on block, and not because of its strike record: `stk_kano` and every `stk_*uppercut` are the retail values to the digit. What does it is the recoil in `kano_blocked`. Blocking costs 8 ticks of stun, so the guard is actionable on tick 9 - but the recoil throws her 73px up for 46 ticks and `away_x_vel 0a000h` walks the gap from 76px to 104px. An uppercut reaches a standing Mileena at 94px and misses from 96px, so the punish fails twice over: swing early and the box goes live under a woman already 40-70px off the floor, wait for her to land and she is 10px past the end of the swing.

**This build:** The recoil is retuned so the whole of her recovery is in range. `rollpush` cuts the horizontal drift (0a000h -> 03000h), landing her at ~82px instead of 104px; `rollgrav` brings her down harder (06000h -> 08000h), dropping the apex from 73px to 55px and the airtime from 46 ticks to 34. She still pops off the guard just as hard - the launch speed is untouched. Nothing about the roll on HIT changes.

**Why:** A special that a correct block does not pay for is the one thing a tournament build cannot leave alone, and this is the only one in the game. Both halves are needed and each fixes a different whiff: with the drift alone, 6 of 8 characters connected at every timing and Liu Kang and Kitana each had ONE dead tick - the swing that goes live at the top of her apex, right on the top edge of the uppercut's 64px band. Gravity is the lever rather than launch speed because apex = v*T/4: cutting the pop costs exactly as much recovery window as it buys, while raising gravity keeps the kick and shortens the fall.

**Evidence:** tools/run_mileena_roll_punish_test.ps1 - scripted roll, crouch-blocked, then an uppercut swept across the recovery. Crouch-block is the best case for the punish on purpose (a standing blocker has to duck first), so a whiff there is a whiff everywhere. Before: 0/7 timings connected, and the range control shows why - the uppercut reaches 94px and she lands at 104px. After: 99/99 across 11 characters at 9 timings each (delays 12-52 ticks after the block), plus 7/7 in the Mileena mirror. The secret fighters are untested here - they cannot be force-selected (only the select cheat reaches them) - but they swing the same `stk_njuppercut`/`stk_fnuppercut` records as Sub-Zero and Kitana.

**Cost / reverting:** `rollpunish 0` restores retail: the roll is safe on block again. `rollpush` and `rollgrav` tune the two halves independently.

### `airswing` = 1

**The arcade:** The drone swings standing attacks at an airborne opponent without ever asking how high he is, in two places. `rt_airborn_close` (MKDRONE.ASM) is the stalk loop's answer to "he is in the air and he is close": pick one of high kick / roundhouse / jab string and throw it, with no test of any kind. `air_rhouse_check`, the wait behind `rh_ooa` and `hk_ooa`, is the whole roster's fallback answer to a jump-in and tests his CLOSEST POINT only - an outstretched leg is inside 040h while the man is 100px up - and it swings when its patience runs out as readily as when the test passes. Every move behind both is pinned at head height and none of them travels up.

**This build:** The two kick paths ask `get_his_dog <= aa_kick_height` first (and `rt_airborn_close` `get_x_dist <= aa_kick_reach` as well), reusing the constants the anti-air styles were already measured onto. A no at `rt_airborn_close` puts the guard up and waits him down, exactly as `stalk_airborn_med` does one band further out; a no at `air_rhouse_check`, including its patience running out, is now simply no swing.

**Why:** A whiffed roundhouse is the most punishable thing the drone can hand back, and it was handing one back for free. Reported against Kung Lao's BLOCKED dive kick, which is where it is most visible rather than where it is worst: `super_rep_blocked` (MKPROP.ASM) parks the victim airborne at gap 0 for the best part of forty ticks, so it meets `rt_airborn_close`'s entry condition every single time, for a third of a second. The intended punish there is fine - `c_hh_sd` walks in and uppercuts - and the roundhouse is a second, unrelated swing on top of it.

**Evidence:** tools/run_divep_ab.ps1 does one side each (set the equate, rebuild, package, sweep 8 seeds) with tools/run_drone_divepunish_test.ps1 driving the dive and scoring HIT/WHIFF on the bar actually moving. Scorpion diff 9. In the blocked-dive window: 1 whiff in 17 swings off, 0 in 93 on. Across every swing at an airborne man the roundhouse goes from 0 of 2 connecting to 6 of 7 - and the UPPERCUT went 15 of 15 and 90 of 90 in the same runs, which is the point: its box rises through the swing and a kick's does not. The change can only remove swings, so the difficulty ladder is untouched: run_drone_antiair_test.ps1 -Diff 0 must still score zero, and does.

**Cost / reverting:** `airswing 0` restores retail, and it is kept deliberately rather than as decoration - the thing being measured is a MISS, and a miss is invisible unless the gate can be turned off and watched to come back. The switch also covers the SWEEP (`drone_sweep`), the third place: a low attack gated on `does_stk_reach` alone, which tests X. With the kick gates in, 52 of the 54 remaining airborne whiffs were sweeps. A height ceiling was measured and rejected - the misses sat LOWER than the hits (23 at dog 27, rising off a blocked dive) - so a man on the floor sweeps as before and an airborne one must be FALLING and no higher than aa_kick_height: all 13 measured hits were oyvel +4..+5 at dog 48-66, and that rule refuses all 33 measured whiffs. On the final build the 8-seed rig saw 3 airborne sweeps, 3 hits, 0 whiffs. `airsweep 0` takes the sweep half out on its own.

## Available, and deliberately OFF

These are built and measured but ship matching the arcade, because
each needs a ruling rather than a fix. The measurement is done so the
ruling can be made on numbers.

### `airzap_once` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** limit air projectiles to one per airborne state

**What is actually there:** built, wired to Mileena's sai, and reverted

**Why it is still off:** It constrained nothing. Reported from play: a player could never throw more than one air projectile per jump anyway, so the rule had no work to do. The sai is spammable because of its input, not because of jumping - see `saicool`.

### `throwbtn` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** give the throw its own input - Low Punch + High Kick - so a close Low Punch on its own is a jab and the throw becomes a decision

**What is actually there:** built and measured; the behaviour lock caught it exactly as designed, `throw_close` going from dmg=34 act=0507h (body slam) to dmg=8 act=0102h (low punch)

**Why it is still off:** It is the PREREQUISITE for charging a whiffed throw, not a goal - and that cost was never built, so with it on the build paid the whole price and collected none of the benefit. Two of the three original complaints about throws turned out to be false as well: the tech already existed, and the P2 throw advantage was a rig defect. A proximity throw is simple but it does not punish good play, which is the line drawn for what should change. Turn it on alongside the whiff cost, not before.

### `airland_recover` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** make an air projectile cost something on landing, by playing the landing animation slower - safe thrown from range, punishable thrown up close

**What is actually there:** built as a rate for `mframew` in `air_land_animation` (MOVES.ASM), used by the tail every air projectile lands through

**Why it is still off:** Tried at 7 ticks per frame against retail's 3 and REPORTED FROM PLAY as still feeling wrong, and as not helping much with what it was for. An earlier attempt that slept after a normal-speed landing was worse - it froze the fighter on the last crouched frame and read as a hang. `airzap_once` is the half that actually addresses the spam and stays on. A landing cost is worth revisiting with a clearer idea of what it should feel like; 7 was a guess inside a recommended band, and a band is not evidence.

### `airzap_minheight` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** refuse an air projectile thrown too close to the ground, killing the instant-air version

**What is actually there:** built into `air_zap_gate` and measured in pixels above the thrower's own ground line

**Why it is still off:** `airzap_once` and `airland_recover` both landed on the same day and neither has been played. A third unplayed change on the same move would make an unwanted result impossible to attribute.

### `firstactive` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** decide a simultaneous exchange by which attack's active window opened first, rather than by which process runs first

**What is actually there:** built, and wrong in three separate ways - it gates on `p_action`, which covers recovery too, so it silently deletes whiff punishing

**Why it is still off:** Beta playtest found it. It should probably be retired rather than repaired: what it implements is not what its name says, and `deferhit` above is a different and better-founded mechanism.

### `perpiece` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** test the strike box against each of the victim's sprite PIECES instead of one collapsed box narrowed to a quarter of its width

**What is actually there:** built and measured, and the measurement is why it is off

**Why it is still off:** It is 99% 'remove the squeeze'. Against a no-squeeze model - the whole silhouette as one box - per-piece differs on 1.02% of verdicts; against the shipping model it differs on 13.66%. So 98% of what it does is widen the target, and the limb precision it was wanted for is the remaining 1%. It also adds ~32px of reach to every move and DELETES the point-blank dead zone across the cast (headband/hikick goes 18..101 to 0..133), which would mean re-cutting all 233 strike records to compensate. Reproduce with: box_model_lab.py flips --models nosqueeze perpiece.

### `stkphase` = 0 (arcade behaviour)

**Would:** let a move's hitbox change shape partway through its ACTIVE window

**What is actually there:** built and inert. Keyed on the striker's a10 countdown, with descending `.word threshold, move_index` pairs; it needs no new process RAM and costs nothing while no move declares phases. One is authored as a worked example - `stk_lklokick_early`, appended at index 17 of `lk_strikes` so indices 0-16 are untouched, giving Liu Kang's low kick 56px of reach for the first half of its window instead of 104.

**Why it is still off:** IT IS NOT WHAT THE 'BOXES ARE ACTIVE TOO EARLY' COMPLAINT WANTS. Those moves already have real startup with no hitbox at all: striker and striker_2 play part one through act_mframew FIRST and only then hold the contact pose for a10 ticks of strike_check, so nothing is checking for a hit during the windup. Phasing splits the ACTIVE window, not the startup, and the art does not change during it - the hold is a single static pose. Measured across the 23 handlers in doc/FRAME_DATA.md section B that report a numeric window: 7 run 1-3 ticks and cannot be meaningfully split, 12 run 4-6 (a 2/2 or 3/3 split), and the only 4 long enough to be interesting - 15 and 16 ticks - are do_flip_kick, do_flip_punch, do_jumpup_kick and do_jumpup_punch. All four are air_strike. A jump attack's box is live for the whole descent because the fighter is committed and travelling, so that length is structural, and phasing it would punch a dead spot into the middle of a jump arc. The long windows are the ones that must NOT be split, and the splittable ones yield 2-3 tick phases.

## What this page does not cover

Added *content* - new moves, characters, stages, fatalities - is a
separate category and is documented per feature in `doc/`. It changes
the game by addition rather than by altering existing behaviour, and a
tournament ruleset would want to list it separately: which characters
are legal, which moves exist that an arcade player will not know.

Also not here: anything measured only in motion. The retail tree is
source and **does not build** (the leak is missing its build tooling,
DIAGCMOS primitives, several labels and 33 of 34 IRW files), so
behaviour that needs a running retail ROM to compare against - the P2
side advantage, throw priority - is measured on this build only and
says so where it is reported.
